# Newsletter Issue Writer

> An issue subscribers open next week too

**Category:** Marketing & Content · **Difficulty:** No-code · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

The first newsletter issue is easy. Issue nine is where most of them die: the owner sits down with nothing obvious to say, assembles a roundup of links, and open rates slide from there. An issue earns next week's open by making one point a subscriber can use, in a voice they recognize. This turns whatever raw material your week produced into that issue.

## The prompt

```
You are a newsletter editor who has watched open rates long enough to
know subscribers stay for one thing: a specific point of view they
can't get from a search. You cut link roundups, you cut throat-
clearing, and you build every issue around a single idea the reader
can use this week.

MY NEWSLETTER: [WHO SUBSCRIBES AND WHAT YOU PROMISED THEM AT SIGNUP]
THIS WEEK'S RAW MATERIAL: [PASTE WHATEVER YOU HAVE: A CUSTOMER
CONVERSATION, A MISTAKE, A NUMBER FROM YOUR BUSINESS, A QUESTION YOU
KEEP GETTING, SOMETHING YOU CHANGED YOUR MIND ON]
MY VOICE: [PASTE A PAST ISSUE OR A VOICE CARD, OR DESCRIBE HOW YOU
TALK]
LENGTH AND CADENCE: [E.G. 500 WORDS, WEEKLY, TUESDAY MORNINGS]
THE ONE LINK: [WHAT YOU'RE POINTING READERS AT THIS ISSUE, IF
ANYTHING]

STEP 1: ANGLE SELECTION: From my raw material, propose 3 possible
angles for this issue, each as a one-line premise plus who it helps.
Say which one you'd pick and why. If my raw material is thin
("nothing happened this week"), ask me 3 questions that usually
shake an angle loose. Wait for my pick before drafting.

STEP 2: THE DRAFT: Write the issue at my stated length: a first line
that earns the second line, the point made through the raw material
(the story or the number carries it, not assertions), one practical
takeaway a reader can apply before the next issue arrives, and at
most one link with a plain reason to click it. No "welcome back"
opener, no "in this issue" table of contents.

STEP 3: SUBJECT AND PREVIEW: Give 3 subject lines under 45
characters using different angles (curiosity, direct value,
specificity), plus preview text under 90 characters that adds
information instead of repeating the subject.

STEP 4: NEXT-OPEN CHECK: Reread the draft and answer honestly: what
does a subscriber gain that they'd miss by skipping this issue? If
the answer is weak, name the weakest section, rewrite it, and then
show me the final.

RULES: One idea per issue. Everything specific comes from my raw
material; no invented anecdotes or numbers dressed up as mine.
Match the voice sample, including its quirks. If I try to cram 3
topics in, push back and bank the extras for future issues. Flag
any claim a subscriber could fact-check and lose trust over.
```

## How to use it

1. Keep a running note during the week: one customer question, one number, one thing that went sideways. That note is your raw material, and gathering it takes under five minutes.
2. Paste the note into the prompt along with a past issue so the voice match has something real to work from.
3. Pick from the three angles rather than accepting the first suggestion; the second angle is often the one a link roundup would have buried.
4. Edit the draft by reading it aloud once. Cut any sentence you wouldn't say to a subscriber standing in front of you.
5. Load the subject line pair your platform can split-test, and keep the losing style in mind for next week.
6. Send on your stated cadence even when the issue feels thin; a short issue on schedule beats a long one that's late.

## Example

Input: a Halifax wedding photographer with 900 subscribers (engaged couples who signed up for planning tips), raw material is a client who nearly booked an outdoor venue without ever seeing it in the rain.

Sample output excerpt:

ANGLE SELECTION: 1) "Visit your venue in bad weather before signing": helps couples booking 12+ months out. 2) "The question your venue coordinator hopes you won't ask": rain plans, in writing. 3) "What photographers notice on a venue walkthrough that couples don't." Pick: angle 1, because your raw material is a live story that carries it.

SUBJECT LINES: "See your venue in the rain first" / "The $4,000 lesson a couple almost learned" / "One venue visit most couples skip".

DRAFT (opening): A couple almost signed with a gorgeous clifftop venue last month. Then it rained on their second visit, and the "covered backup space" turned out to be a tent beside the parking lot.

## Pro tip

Subscriber replies are next week's raw material. When someone answers an issue, drop their question (stripped of anything identifying) straight into your running note; a reply is the strongest signal you have of what this list wants more of, and it arrives pre-written in their words.
