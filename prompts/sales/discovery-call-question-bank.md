# Discovery Call Question Bank

> Questions that surface budget and urgency without interrogating

**Category:** Sales & Lead Generation · **Difficulty:** No-code · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

Ask "what's your budget?" in the first ten minutes and most prospects go vague on you. Skip the money questions entirely and you spend three weeks courting someone who could not afford you on day one. Generic discovery question lists don't help because they ignore sequencing: the same question lands as curiosity or as interrogation depending on what came before it. This builds a question bank ordered so the hard questions feel earned.

## The prompt

```
You are a sales coach who has listened to hundreds of recorded discovery
calls and can tell within five minutes whether a rep is having a
conversation or conducting an audit. You believe budget and urgency get
surfaced through indirect questions first, and that a question list
without sequencing is a script for an interrogation.

MY BUSINESS: [WHAT YOU SELL, TYPICAL DEAL SIZE, WHO USUALLY BUYS]
MY CURRENT DISCOVERY APPROACH: [WHAT YOU ASK NOW AND WHERE CALLS GO
WRONG: E.G. "PROSPECTS GO QUIET WHEN I ASK ABOUT BUDGET" OR "CALLS RUN
LONG AND I STILL DON'T KNOW IF THEY'RE SERIOUS"]

STEP 1: FIT CHECK. Ask me what a great-fit client looks like and what a
bad-fit client looks like, with one real example of each if I have them.
If my answer is "anyone who pays," push back: a question bank built for
everyone surfaces nothing. Don't move on until we've named 2-3 signals
that separate serious buyers from browsers in my market.

STEP 2: THE BANK. Build 15-20 questions in five labeled groups:
- CONTEXT: 3-4 openers that get them talking about their situation.
- PAIN: 3-4 that surface the specific problem and its cost in time or
  money, with one follow-up each for shallow answers.
- MONEY: 3-4 that reveal budget range indirectly before any direct ask
  (what they spend now, what the problem costs them, what they've paid
  for similar work before).
- URGENCY: 3-4 that separate "this quarter" from "someday" without
  asking "when do you want to start."
- DECISION: 2-3 that map who else signs off and what would kill the
  deal.

STEP 3: SEQUENCE AND TRANSITIONS. Order the groups for a 30-minute
call, then write the transition sentence between each group so the
shift doesn't feel like a gear change. Mark the 2-3 questions I can
drop if the call runs short.

RULES: Every question must be open-ended and sayable out loud by a
normal person. Ban the cliches: no "what keeps you up at night," no "if
you had a magic wand." Never open with a direct budget question; the
MONEY group earns the direct version only if the indirect ones stall.
If my deal size is under about $1,000, say so and cut the bank to 8-10
questions, because a long discovery call costs more than it's worth at
that price. End with the 3 questions that matter most if I remember
nothing else on the call.
```

## How to use it

1. Fill in your business and be honest about where your discovery calls fall apart today. The bank gets built around that weakness.
2. Answer the fit-check questions with real examples of a great client and a bad one, names optional.
3. Read the full bank once, then cut anything you'd feel awkward saying out loud; a question you won't ask is dead weight.
4. Print the sequenced version or keep it on a second screen for your next three calls, treating it as a spine, not a script.
5. After each call, note which questions got useful answers and which got fluff, then ask the AI to replace the fluff producers.

## Example

Input: a 5-person IT support company in Halifax selling managed service contracts around $2,000/month to professional offices.

Sample output excerpt:

MONEY (indirect first): 1) "What does IT support look like for you today: someone in-house, a person you call, or whoever's nearest when something breaks?" 2) "When the network went down last time, what did that day cost you, roughly, in billable time?" 3) "What are you spending now across all of it: the ad-hoc calls, the software subscriptions, the hours your office manager loses to printer issues?" Follow-up if vague: "Even a rough monthly figure helps me tell you whether we'd be a fit or an overkill."

TRANSITION into URGENCY: "That's useful context on the costs. Can I ask what made this the week you took the call, rather than three months ago?"

## Pro tip

Run the finished bank through the AI a second time and ask: "For each MONEY and URGENCY question, write the answer a bad-fit prospect would give." Knowing what a polite no sounds like is half the value; you'll stop hearing "send me some info" as encouragement.
