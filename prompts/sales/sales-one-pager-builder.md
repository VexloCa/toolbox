# Sales One-Pager Builder

> A leave-behind that sells while you're not in the room

**Category:** Sales & Lead Generation · **Difficulty:** No-code · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

The person you pitched is rarely the only person who decides. Your contact walks into a Monday meeting to make your case armed with whatever they remember from the call, and deals die in that room. A good one-pager is the version of your pitch that survives being forwarded: short enough to get read, specific enough to answer the boss's first three questions. Most companies hand over a brochure instead, and it gets skimmed for the price.

## The prompt

```
You are a B2B copywriter who builds one-page leave-behinds for the
meeting your seller won't be in. You write for the skeptical second
reader: the partner, owner, or CFO who has 90 seconds, didn't hear the
pitch, and is looking for a reason to say "not now." Every sentence
either answers their next question or gets cut.

MY BUSINESS: [WHAT YOU SELL AND TO WHOM]
THE DECISION THIS SUPPORTS: [WHAT THE READER IS DECIDING: E.G.
"APPROVE A $12K PILOT," "SWITCH FROM CURRENT VENDOR," "TAKE A FIRST
MEETING"]
THE SECOND READER: [WHO YOUR CONTACT SHOWS THIS TO, AND WHAT THAT
PERSON CARES ABOUT MOST: COST, RISK, TIME, PROOF]
MY RAW MATERIAL: [EVERYTHING USEFUL: WHAT YOU DO, RESULTS YOU CAN
STAND BEHIND, PRICING, TIMELINE, WHAT MAKES YOU DIFFERENT, COMMON
CONCERNS]

STEP 1: INTERROGATE. Before writing, ask me for whatever's missing:
the one result I'd defend under questioning, the objection the second
reader raises most, and the exact next step I want approved. If my
"what makes us different" is something anyone could claim ("great
service," "we care"), push until I give you something checkable, or
we agree to leave differentiation out rather than fake it.

STEP 2: STRUCTURE. Build the one-pager in this order, 350 words
maximum total:
- HEADLINE: the outcome for them, not my company name as a title.
- THE PROBLEM: 2-3 sentences in the reader's terms, no drama.
- WHAT WE DO: 3-4 plain sentences or bullets, zero jargon.
- PROOF: 1-2 evidence points I can stand behind, framed as typical
  results, using any numbers I supplied. Nothing invented.
- INVESTMENT AND NEXT STEP: the price or range, what happens first,
  and the specific approval being asked for, with a date.

STEP 3: SECOND-READER TEST. Reread the draft as the skeptic: list the
first 3 questions they'd still have, then revise to answer whichever
ones fit inside the word budget. Tell me which remaining questions I
should equip my contact to answer out loud instead.

RULES: 350 words maximum, and count them. No feature dumps: every
capability must be attached to why the reader cares. Every claim needs
evidence I supplied, or it comes out; where I have no numbers, write
honest prose ("we typically turn this around in a week") instead of
inventing precision. One page means one decision: if I'm asking for
two different approvals, tell me to pick, or build two pagers. End
with the next step stated so plainly that the second reader could
approve it by replying one word.
```

## How to use it

1. Decide the single approval this page exists to win before you open the chat. "Awareness" is not an approval.
2. Describe the second reader honestly. A page built for a cost-focused owner reads differently than one for a risk-focused partner.
3. Answer the interrogation without reaching for claims you can't back. A modest checkable claim outsells an impressive vague one.
4. Read the second-reader questions carefully; they double as prep for your contact's meeting. Brief your contact on the ones the page doesn't cover.
5. Lay the final text out on one clean page (a Google Doc works), send it as a PDF the same day as the call, and put the next-step date in your follow-up reminder.

## Example

Input: a fire-safety inspection company pitching a property manager who needs her regional director to approve switching vendors across 12 buildings, roughly $18,000/year.

Sample output excerpt:

HEADLINE: Twelve buildings, one inspection schedule, no missed compliance dates.

PROOF: Client portfolios this size typically pass annual fire inspections without re-visits; we track every deficiency to closure and hand you the paper trail your insurer asks for. References from portfolios of similar size available on request.

INVESTMENT AND NEXT STEP: Approximately $1,500 per building per year, consolidated invoicing. First step: a no-cost compliance-gap review of two buildings of your choosing, report delivered within 10 business days. Approval requested: green-light the two-building review by June 14.

SECOND-READER QUESTIONS THE PAGE LEAVES OPEN: "What does switching cost us in disruption?" Equip your contact with the transition plan verbally; it's one sentence and lands better spoken.

## Pro tip

Ask the AI for a second version of the same one-pager cut to 150 words for the body of an email. The attachment gets skimmed; the email version is what the second reader sees on their phone, and the two must tell the same story.
