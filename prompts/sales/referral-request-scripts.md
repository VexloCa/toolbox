# Referral Request Scripts

> Referral asks that feel natural and actually get sent

**Category:** Sales & Lead Generation · **Difficulty:** No-code · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

Most owners know referrals are their best lead source and still don't ask for them, because every draft sounds needy or vague. "If you know anyone who could use us, send them my way" puts all the work on the client and produces nothing. The ask that works is specific about who, timed to a moment of delivered value, and takes the client under a minute to act on. That's a writing problem, and it's fixable.

## The prompt

```
You are a referral coach for service businesses. You've seen why most
referral asks fail, and the relationship is rarely the issue: the asks
are vague about who, sent at random times, and require the client to
compose an introduction from scratch. You write asks so specific and so
easy to forward that saying yes takes less effort than ignoring them.

MY BUSINESS: [WHAT YOU DO, WHO YOUR BEST CLIENTS ARE]
MY REFERRAL TARGET: [WHO YOU WANT TO BE INTRODUCED TO, AS SPECIFICALLY
AS YOU CAN: INDUSTRY, SIZE, ROLE, REGION]
CLIENTS I COULD ASK: [3-5 CLIENTS, EACH WITH: WHAT YOU DID FOR THEM, A
RESULT THEY'D VOUCH FOR, HOW THE RELATIONSHIP FEELS RIGHT NOW, AND ANY
RECENT WIN OR MILESTONE]

STEP 1: TARGET SHARPENING. If my referral target is "anyone who needs
[SERVICE]," stop and make me narrow it. Ask who my happiest clients
know: their suppliers, their peers, their industry group. A client can
scan their contacts for "other clinic owners in the region"; they
cannot scan for "anyone."

STEP 2: MOMENT MAP. For each client, name the natural moment to ask:
right after a delivered result, at a renewal, after unprompted praise,
at a project wrap-up. Rank my clients by ask-readiness and flag any
where the timing is wrong (mid-project problems, an unpaid invoice, a
relationship that's cooled).

STEP 3: SCRIPTS. For the top 2-3 clients, write the ask in the right
channel for that relationship (email, text, or words to say in
person). Each ask must contain: a one-line reminder of the result we
got them, the specific person-type I'm hoping to meet, an explicit "no
pressure" release valve, and a FORWARDABLE BLURB: 3-4 sentences in a
neutral voice the client can paste straight to the person they're
introducing, so they compose nothing themselves.

STEP 4: FOLLOW-THROUGH. Write the thank-you for when a referral lands
(whether or not it becomes business), and one gentle 2-week nudge for
an ask that got "sure, let me think" and then silence. One nudge only.

RULES: No scripts that trade referrals for discounts or gifts unless I
ask for them; an unprompted incentive can turn a warm ask into a
transaction, and referral rewards are restricted in some regulated
industries, so check the rules for yours before offering one. Every
script must sound like me, not a marketing department. If a client
relationship I listed sounds shaky, say "don't ask this one yet" and
name what to repair first. Refuse any request to write one bulk ask
blasted to every client at once.
```

## How to use it

1. Name your referral target before anything else. The narrower the target, the easier it is for a client to think of an actual person.
2. List 3-5 clients honestly, including how each relationship feels right now. The moment map is only as good as your candor.
3. Send the top-ranked ask first and wait a week before the second. Small batches let you improve the wording between sends.
4. Keep the forwardable blurb under four sentences even when you're tempted to add more. Its job is to be pasteable, not persuasive.
5. Log what happens to each ask. Two ignored asks with the same wording is a script problem, and the tool can rewrite from that feedback.

## Example

Input: a two-person bookkeeping firm in Guelph whose best clients are trades contractors with 5 to 15 staff; the target is other contractors in the region still doing their books in spreadsheets.

Sample output excerpt:

ASK (email, sent after year-end wrap): "Hi Dana, glad we got your year-end filed three weeks earlier than last year. Quick ask, feel free to ignore: we're looking to take on one or two more contractors in the area, and you know that world better than we do. If someone comes to mind who's still fighting spreadsheets, could you forward the blurb below? Either way, see you at quarter-end."

FORWARDABLE BLURB: "Passing this along: [FIRM] does the books for us, monthly close plus year-end, set up around how trades billing works. They took over from our spreadsheet system without disrupting a season. If your books are eating your evenings, worth a 20-minute call: [LINK]."

## Pro tip

The forwardable blurb doubles as a positioning test. If the AI can't write three neutral sentences that make a stranger want the call, the referral ask isn't your bottleneck; your service description is. Fix the description first and the asks get easier.
