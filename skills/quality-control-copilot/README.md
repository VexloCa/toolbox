# Quality Control Copilot

> Growth without the quality slide, caught by spot checks instead of complaints

**Category:** Operations & Productivity · **Difficulty:** Light setup · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

Quality slides when a business grows, and it slides quietly. The founder used to touch every job; now three people do the work and the first sign of trouble is a refund request or a 2-star review. Writing the process down was step one ([SOP Studio](/toolbox/sop-studio) covers it), but a document nobody checks against is a hope, not a control. What growing teams are missing is the boring middle layer: what gets checked, how often, by whom, and what happened last time.

## The skill

The installable file is [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md) in this folder. Add it to your assistant's skills folder, or paste it at the start of a conversation.

## How to use it

1. Click "Install skill" on this page and copy the install prompt.
2. Paste it into your AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, anything that accepts pasted text).
3. Name the 2 or 3 processes where a miss costs you customers, and answer its questions about what "done right" looks like.
4. Run the first spot checks yourself, then hand the checklist to a lead. Log results in a one-line paste.
5. Read the monthly one-pager and make the one fix it recommends before adding new checks.

## Example

A 9-person cleaning company grows from 20 to 45 weekly jobs and the callbacks creep up. The skill builds a 6-point exit checklist per job type and a plan to spot-check 3 jobs a week, rotating crews. Four weeks of one-line logs later, the pattern reads: bathroom glass fails mostly on jobs booked under 2 hours. The fix it drafts isn't retraining, it's a booking rule (glass-heavy homes get the longer slot), plus one line added to the SOP. Callbacks drop the next month, and the owner saw it in the log before any customer said it in a review.

## Pro tip

Cap every checklist at 7 items even when 15 feel important. A long checklist gets skimmed, and the skill's pattern log will tell you which dropped items earn their way back on. Delegating the checks themselves is its own process; the [Delegation Planner](/toolbox/delegation-planner) pairs well when the founder stops being the checker.
