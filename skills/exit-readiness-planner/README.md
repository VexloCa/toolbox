# Exit Readiness Planner

> An honest read of what a buyer or a successor would find in your business today, the eight things that move the price most, and a twelve-month plan to fix them before anyone looks

**Category:** Strategy & Planning · **Difficulty:** Light setup · **Works with:** ChatGPT, Claude

## The problem

A lot of Canadian owners plan to sell or hand over their business in the next decade, and most of them have never looked at it the way a buyer would. A buyer sees a business that stops when the owner takes a week off, a customer list where one account is 40 percent of revenue, books that need "adjustments" to show the real profit, a lease with eighteen months left and no assignment clause, and a crew whose knowledge lives in their heads. Each of those costs money at the table, and each takes a year or two to fix. The broker and the accountant come in at the end. The work that changes the price happens now. This skill is the buyer's walk-through, three years early.

## How to use it

1. Click "Install skill" on this page and copy the install prompt.
2. Paste it into your AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, anything that accepts pasted text).
3. Answer the interview honestly; the point is to hear the hard questions from a machine before you hear them from a buyer.
4. Read the ranked fixes and pick the first two; put the quarterly milestones in your calendar.
5. Re-run the readiness read every six months and watch the areas move.

## Example

A 14-person commercial landscaping company in Kingston, owner 58, plans to sell in about three years. The interview is uncomfortable in the right places: the owner quotes every job personally, one condominium corporation is 38 percent of revenue, the books are kept by a relative and the year-end is a compilation, and the yard lease ends in 20 months. The readiness read scores owner dependence and concentration as the two things a buyer would discount hardest. The twelve-month plan starts with training the senior foreman to quote the three most common job types and taking a real two-week absence in July, then chasing two more condo boards to bring the big one under 25 percent, then a conversation with the accountant about review-engagement statements for the next two year-ends. The data room folder starts with eleven documents and a list of nine that don't exist yet.

## Pro tip

Take the thirty days before you fix anything else. The list of what broke while you were gone is the most accurate readiness report you'll ever get, and it costs nothing but the nerve to leave. And start the lease conversation two years out, not two months: a landlord who knows a sale is coming is a landlord who can price it in.
