# The Idea Grill

> Five hostile experts interrogate your idea and score it out of 100 before you build

**Category:** Strategy & Planning · **Difficulty:** No-code · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

The most expensive mistake in small business is building something nobody asked for, and it usually starts the same way: an idea that only ever gets discussed with people who are nice to you. Your spouse says it sounds great. Your friend says they'd buy it. Then you spend three months and real money finding out strangers disagree. What's missing isn't feedback. It's interrogation, from someone with no stake in your feelings.

## The skill

The installable file is [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md) in this folder. Add it to your assistant's skills folder, or paste it at the start of a conversation.

## How to use it

1. Click "Install skill" on this page and copy the install prompt.
2. Paste it into Claude or ChatGPT and answer the five setup questions honestly, including what you'd charge. A guess is fine.
3. Let each panelist finish their questions before defending your idea. Answer "I don't know" when you don't; that's data.
4. Take the scorecard's three cheapest tests and actually run them this week. Each is designed to cost under $100.
5. Come back with results and say "re-grill me": only the affected panelists re-run, and the score moves on evidence only.

## Example

A bookkeeper wants to launch a $99/month "quarterly tax-readiness check" for freelancers. The Unconvinced Customer asks when a freelancer last felt tax pain. Answer: April. Follow-up: so why would they pay in August? No good answer. Demand: 4/10. The Bookkeeper panelist finds solid margins (9/10). The Growth Skeptic asks where the first ten customers come from; "Instagram" doesn't survive the follow-up. Distribution: 3/10, with an unknown named: no channel where freelancers feel tax pain year-round. Total: 51/100, real idea with named holes. Cheapest test: email 20 past clients in September offering the check at $79, count who bites. That one test would move Demand and Distribution both.

## Pro tip

Run the grill twice: once on your idea, once on the version of it you'd build if you had half the budget. When the smaller version scores higher, and it often does, that gap is telling you what the idea actually is.
