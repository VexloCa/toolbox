# Local Press Pitch Writer

> A short, specific pitch to a named local reporter or trade editor, built around a story they'd run with or without you, plus the follow-up and the quote sheet

**Category:** Marketing & Content · **Difficulty:** Light setup · **Works with:** ChatGPT, Claude

## The problem

A mention in the local paper, the regional business weekly or your trade's magazine still does something a hundred social posts don't: it gets read by people who weren't looking for you, and it stays on Google for years. Most owners never pitch because they assume you need an agency, or they pitch once with "we're excited to announce our new location" and never hear back. Reporters don't run announcements. They run stories, and a story has a reason to exist this week, a person in it, and a number. Your business has three of those a year. This skill finds them and writes the pitch a reporter can use.

## How to use it

1. Click "Install skill" on this page and copy the install prompt.
2. Paste it into your AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, anything that accepts pasted text).
3. Answer the setup questions once: your business, your area, your trade publications, and anything newsworthy in the past year.
4. When something happens, or a season or regulation is coming, run the angle finder and let it write the pitch.
5. Find the reporter by name on the beat it suggests, send the pitch, and send the follow-up a week later.

## Example

A family-run garden centre in Peterborough wants coverage for its spring opening, which is an announcement, and the skill says so. The angle finder surfaces something better: the owner has noticed first-time vegetable gardeners up sharply since grocery prices climbed, and the centre tracked it (seed-starting kits sold out twice last April). That's a local story with a number and a trend the regional paper has already covered from the grocery side. The pitch offers the owner for an interview and the sales figures by week. The reporter writes a piece about food costs and backyard gardens, the centre is the example, and the article still ranks for "vegetable garden Peterborough" a year later.

## Pro tip

Pitch the reporter who wrote about your topic in the last month, by name, and reference the piece in your first line. A general newsroom inbox is where pitches go to die. And when they say yes, be available that day. A local reporter's deadline is this afternoon, and the business that picks up the phone is the one in the story.
