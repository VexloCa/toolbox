# Shift Scheduler Copilot

> The weekly schedule builds itself from memory; you make the judgment calls

**Category:** HR & Hiring · **Difficulty:** Light setup · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

The [Shift Schedule Builder](/toolbox/shift-schedule-builder) solves one week, and for one week it is enough. But scheduling is not a weekly puzzle; it is a running story. Who covered the last three Saturday closes, whose availability changed when school started, which swap Dana still owes Marcus, who is quietly drifting toward overtime every second week: none of that fits in a paste, and all of it is what makes a schedule feel fair or slowly poison a team. This skill installs the scheduler who remembers.

## The skill

The installable file is [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md) in this folder. Add it to your assistant's skills folder, or paste it at the start of a conversation.

## How to use it

1. Click "Install skill" on this page and copy the install prompt.
2. Paste it into your AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, anything that accepts pasted text).
3. Do the roster interview once, hard rules included.
4. Each week, say what changed and ask for the build; send the asks before posting.
5. Report swaps and callouts as they happen, two words is enough ("Dana covered Marcus").

## Example

A restaurant with fourteen staff installs the skill and runs it for a month. Week one is the interview plus a first build. By week three, the owner types one line ("build next week, Sam requested Friday off") and gets the draft with a note that Sam also had last Friday off while Jess covered both, so the suggested trade runs the other way this time. In week four a callout gets logged, and the "who to ask" list leads with the two people who owe covers. At the month's end, the pattern flag is blunt: Tuesday close has had exactly one qualified person available for five straight weeks, and that is a cross-training or hiring decision, not a scheduling trick.

## Pro tip

Log the tiny stuff, especially covers and swaps, the moment they happen; the fairness ledger is only as good as its entries, and "the schedule finally feels fair" is the single loudest thing staff say when it works. When a pattern flag names the same person three weeks running, take it to a real conversation early; the [Tough Conversation Scripts](/toolbox/tough-conversation-scripts) exist for exactly the talk you are tempted to let the schedule keep absorbing.
