# Business Plan Copilot

> A working business plan and financial forecast, built section by section

**Category:** Strategy & Planning · **Difficulty:** Light setup · **Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT

## The problem

Most business plans get written twice: once in a template-filling panic before a loan application, and never again. The document runs 30 pages, the numbers were invented at midnight, and by month three it describes a business that no longer exists. What a small business actually needs is shorter and harder: a plan where every number is labeled fact or guess, a forecast that shows its assumptions, and a document that gets revised when reality argues with it.

## The skill

The installable file is [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md) in this folder. Add it to your assistant's skills folder, or paste it at the start of a conversation.

## How to use it

1. Click "Install skill" on this page and copy the install prompt.
2. Paste it into your AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, anything that accepts pasted text).
3. Answer the setup questions: the business, the audience for the plan (you, a lender, a partner), and what records exist.
4. Work through the sections across a few sittings; a session per section beats one heroic evening.
5. When reality diverges from the plan, come back and say what changed. That conversation is the whole point.

## Example

A couple opening a second bakery location works through the skill over a week. The interview surfaces that their "we'll double revenue" assumption rests on the new neighbourhood matching the old one's foot traffic, which nobody has measured; the forecast tags that row ESTIMATE with a note to count passersby for three mornings before signing the lease. The finished one-pager and 12-month forecast go to their bank contact reshaped into the formal layout, with the FACT rows traceable to their point-of-sale export. The lease decision waits two weeks for the foot-traffic count, and the count changes the revenue estimate down 20%, which they take to their accountant before committing.

## Pro tip

Build the forecast before the mission statement. Working through the numbers first surfaces the questions the prose sections should answer, and it keeps the writing honest: prose written after the math describes the business you have, not the one you wish you had. Pressure-test the finished plan with the [Business Model Stress Test](/toolbox/business-model-stress-test) before anyone external sees it.
